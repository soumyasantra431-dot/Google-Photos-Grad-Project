# Six short retrieval interviews — moderator kit

Use for 5–6 **real** participants. One interview = one recent, specific retrieval task. Target 15–20 minutes; do not promise a particular outcome. Use participant codes P01–P06. Do not enter invented answers or copy personal photos into the research file.

## Recruitment message to send through the original survey channel

> I am studying how people find one older photo they remember in Google Photos. If you use Photos and have recently tried to find a specific older photo without knowing its exact date or album, would you join a 15–20 minute call? You can choose a safe, non-sensitive photo and keep your screen private whenever you wish. I will record notes about your search steps, not your photos. I may also invite you to try a prototype in a separate short session.

Recruit a mix of people whose first attempt succeeded, failed, or took effort. Do not screen in only failures. Seek variation in *task situation*: personal/event memory, functional artifact, and photo resurfaced in Memories or another surface. A person can have more than one situation, so code the task rather than assigning a permanent persona. If a respondent cannot safely show a real library, conduct an incident-reconstruction interview and mark `task_observed: no` rather than inventing observed actions.

## Moderator script

**0–2 min · Consent and target.** “Is it okay if I take anonymous notes about your search steps? Please do not show anything sensitive. What exact photo did you have in mind, and why did you want it?” Confirm it is one known item, not a generic category search.

**2–5 min · Memory before search.** “Without opening Photos yet, tell me everything you remember about that photo. What do you not remember?” Let the person speak first; only then probe person, object, appearance, event, place, approximate time, surrounding event, text in image, album, and source/device.

**5–12 min · Attempt.** “Please try to find that exact photo as you normally would, narrating only what you are comfortable sharing.” Record the first action and exact query, which surface they used (classic Search, Ask Photos, Memories, timeline, album, other), which results they inspected, whether the target was visible, every reformulation, and the confirmed outcome. Avoid suggesting a query or clue.

**12–17 min · Debrief.** “What did you expect the first search to show? What made a result look promising? What did you try next and why? If you did not find it, what would you normally do after this?” Ask whether the target was absent, present but hard to recognize, or unknown; do not infer technical root cause from the person's explanation alone.

Use **Five Whys only on a concrete moment** in the observed or recalled task: “What made you try that query?”, “Why did that result seem wrong?”, “Why did you switch to the timeline?”, and so on. Follow the participant's actual answers; do not force five questions or pre-fill a causal chain. Record competing explanations (e.g. absent candidate versus unrecognized candidate) when the evidence does not resolve them.

**17–20 min · Follow-up.** “Would you be willing to try a small prototype in a separate 10-minute session?” Record permission only; do not claim a return test has happened yet.

## Synthesis guardrails

- Separate *reported* past behavior from what was *observed* in the session.
- Distinguish “not mentioned” from “explicitly forgotten.”
- Distinguish the user's exact query from the researcher's paraphrase or the user's wished-for query.
- Do not equate a missing result with proven language-model failure; indexing, ranking, availability, and recognition are rivals.
- Report participant-level findings as raw counts and cases, not population percentages.
- If mock transcripts are created to rehearse this kit, label them `simulated`, keep them outside the findings dataset, and never cite them as interviews.
